Why our templates don't feel premium yet, and how to close the gap

You're right. I put Harbour, Signal and Corner next to Eliras, Zorven and Labzzy at the same width. The gap is real, and it has two causes: what Crust can express at all, and how the three templates use it.

Where the gap comes from

~60% the system: fonts, type sizes, blocks, hero layouts, motion~40% the templates: length, copy, image consistency

Better copy alone won't get us there. Every template we make, free or paid, can only look as good as the blocks and style options allow. So the system has to come first.

What we have today

Our three home pages at 1440 px

The three references run 7,700 to 26,000 px with 7 to 19 sections. Ours stop at about a third of that, so they read as outlines rather than finished sites.

What makes the references feel premium

I'm learning principles from these sites, not copying them. They're other designers' paid work, so our templates keep their own layouts, copy and photos.

B · Upgrade the design layer, then rebuild the templates

Give Crust what premium templates are made of, then remake Harbour, Signal and Corner on it.

  • Fonts: about 20 open-licence typefaces bundled with Crust and served from the site itself (free, private, fast)
  • Type and details: display sizes and tight tracking, mono labels, tag chips
  • Hero layouts: three arrangements, including stats and a floating card
  • New blocks: Stats, Numbered list, Image strip, Pricing; Logo row and Quote get richer styles
  • Motion: CSS only, off with Reduce motion, content visible without it
  • Stricter template check: catches filler text, empty slots and mismatched crops

Why B, and what happens next

The references feel premium because of a type system, a section vocabulary and finished content. None of them depends on free positioning, so C adds risk without adding what we're missing. A only treats the symptoms.

  1. I keep building F063, the store, now. It doesn't depend on this, and it will show whatever the templates become.
  2. If you approve B, I write it up as a milestone ("Premium design layer") with its features, for you to approve, and it goes before the premium templates track.
  3. Accessibility stays a requirement: small mono labels keep 4.5:1 contrast and at least 12 px, text on photos gets a scrim, and motion never hides content.
