Crust · F048 · design snapshot

Start from a template

Pick a template, look at every page at three widths, then replace your site with it, knowing exactly what changes and how to get your old site back.

Three directions

A · Gallery screen

“Start from a template” on the Pages screen opens its own screen: a grid of templates, then a full-width preview with the page list beside it. The Back button works and the preview gets the whole width, so the desktop view is readable.

Cost: one more screen to learn, but it behaves like Posts and Site settings already do.

B · Dialog on Pages

A large dialog over the Pages screen with the template list on the left and the preview on the right. Feels quick and keeps you on Pages.

Cost: the desktop preview is squeezed into a dialog, the confirm question becomes a dialog inside a dialog (confusing for screen reader users), and on a phone it turns into a full screen anyway.

C · Three steps

One thing at a time: 1 Choose, 2 Look through the pages, 3 Confirm. Very calm on a phone.

Cost: comparing two templates means stepping back and forth; more clicks for something you'll do rarely but carefully.

Asking first

“Use this template” opens this question. Focus starts on Cancel; Escape closes it. The list is worked out from your site, so it names your actual pages.

Replace your site with “Harbour”?

Replaced

  • Your 3 pages: Home, About, Menu
  • Colours, fonts and styles
  • Header and footer
  • Your 2 blog posts and the blog’s title
  • Your linked section “Contact strip”

Kept

  • Site name and address
  • Your photo library (9 photos added)
  • My layouts (2 added)
  • The live site, until you publish

Your current site stays in the history. The Pages screen offers “Bring back my previous site” afterwards.

Your site now uses the “Harbour” template.

Keyboard, screen readers, phone

WhereHow it works
GalleryEach template is one button; Tab moves between them, Enter opens the preview. Focus moves to the template's name heading.
Page listButtons with aria-current on the page shown; the preview's title says which page and width (“Harbour, About, phone width”).
WidthsToggle buttons (aria-pressed), 44 px tall. On a phone the preview starts at phone width.
PreviewA sandboxed frame with no scripts, scaled to fit, rendered exactly as publishing renders it. Photos come from inside the template file.
ConfirmA modal dialog (alertdialog), focus on Cancel, Escape cancels. Replace is red because it replaces.
ResultAnnounced on the Pages screen; focus goes to its heading.

Why A, and why the confirm step looks like this

  • Room for the preview. The decision is visual; a desktop page squeezed into a dialog misrepresents the template (direction B's main problem).
  • Error prevention plus undo (Nielsen #5 and #3). Replacing a site is rare and big, so it asks once with specifics, and it can be undone. A typed confirmation would add friction without adding safety, because undo exists.
  • “Replaced” next to “Kept”. People mostly worry about what they'd lose; naming what survives (photos, layouts, the live site) answers that before it's asked.
  • Nothing goes live. Applying changes the draft site only; the live site changes when you publish, same as every other change.
