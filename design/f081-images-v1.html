Crust · F081 · design snapshot · 2 Nov 2026

Lighter photos, and crops that keep the subject

Two things in this feature. Publishing makes AVIF and WebP copies of every photo, with nothing to set. And each photo in the library gets a focal point, so when a card, gallery or hero crops it, the important part stays in frame. Only the focal point picker has a visible design choice; it's below.

1 · Automatic, nothing to choose

The same photo, three formats

Real numbers from Corner's carnations.jpg (1280 × 1600), made by the new publish step. Browsers that read AVIF (over 90% of visitors) get the smallest; older ones get WebP, then the JPEG. Every smaller copy for phones gets the same treatment.

3 · Decision · how to pick the point

In the media library's photo details

Each option replaces the large preview in the details panel (right side of the library, under the photo grid on a phone). Try them: they work.

A · Recommended

Press on the photo, see the crops

Focal point
Press the part that must stay in view. Arrow keys move the point; Shift moves it further.
Wide
Square
Tall
  • Direct: you point at the subject, the way Figma and Squarespace do it.
  • The three small crops answer "what will a card or phone show?" before you leave the library.
  • Any point, not just nine.

Access: one tab stop; arrows move 5% (Shift 20%); the label reads the position as text. Pressing a spot is a single click, so no dragging is needed (WCAG 2.5.7). The dot has a white ring and a dark ring, visible on light and dark photos (3:1, WCAG 1.4.11).

B

Nine positions

Focal point
Pick which part of the photo matters most.
  • Simplest to understand, nothing to aim.
  • Coarse: a subject between two positions gets clipped (here the guitarist sits between left-middle and centre).
  • No preview of the crops unless we add them.

Access: a radio group, arrows move between the nine; easy for screen readers ("Left, middle, selected"). Targets are 40 px, above the 24 px minimum.

C

Two sliders

Focal point
  • Precise, and standard controls.
  • Indirect: you move two bars to steer a dot, the opposite of "point at it".
  • Takes the most room in a panel that also holds alt text.

Access: native sliders, so keyboard and screen readers work with no extra code.
