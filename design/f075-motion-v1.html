Milestone 8 · F075 Motion · design sketch

Pages that move a little, and only when it helps

Sections ease in as you scroll, cards follow one after another, photos drift slowly behind the text, cards lift under the pointer, and a logo row can scroll slowly with a pause button. Pure CSS: no scripts on the page. Below, each effect plays in a small sample page. After that come three ways to set it up in the builder.

Your device has Reduce motion turned on, so these samples stay still. Published sites behave the same way for visitors who turn it on.

On the published page

Where each effect lands

1 · Entrance

Quiet rooms by the harbour
Book a stay

When a section scrolls into view, its blocks rise into place and its photos fade in. Choices: Rise, Zoom, Slide in, or None. Built: words never fade (see below). The first section is already on screen when the page opens, so it never waits.

2 · Stagger

Columns, grid cards and gallery photos come in one after another, about a tenth of a second apart, from left to right. That's the "designed" rhythm premium sites have.

3 · Light parallax

Scroll this frame

A section's background photo moves a little slower than the page. It's a light effect: the photo shifts about 8% of its height. The text never moves. Only for photo backgrounds.

4 · Hover lift

Point at a card (or Tab to it): it lifts 3 px and its photo zooms in slightly. Buttons lift too. Only cards and buttons that link somewhere move, so the effect tells you what you can press.

5 · Moving logo row

A logo row can scroll slowly and endlessly. It stops while the pointer or keyboard is on it, and it always has a pause button: anything that moves by itself for more than 5 seconds needs one (WCAG 2.2.2). Screen readers read each logo once.

6 · Always safe

Reduce motion
Old browser
No motion set

Content is never hidden waiting for an effect. With Reduce motion on, in browsers that can't link animation to scrolling (Firefox today), or on sites that never turn motion on, the page shows complete and still, exactly as now.

  • One decision covers the whole site. Off, Subtle or Lively sets the entrance, stagger, timing and hover lift together. Premium sites use one motion language throughout; mixing effects is what makes a page look like a slideshow. This is the consistency heuristic (Nielsen #4).
  • Exceptions where they're useful. A section can use another entrance or none (a price table that shouldn't wait). Parallax and the moving logos are opt-in where they apply.
  • Three levels to choose from, not a dozen settings. Timing is a craft decision, so Crust makes it. You compare finished looks instead of typing durations (Hick's law; recognition over recall).
  • Old sites stay still. Missing means Off. The style library's looks can suggest a level later.

How it stays fast and accessible

No scripts
Effects use CSS scroll-linked animation. Pages stay script-free, and Lighthouse stays at 95 or more.
Words never fade
Changed while building. Scroll-linked effects follow the scroll, so text that faded in would stay half see-through if a visitor stopped scrolling with it near the bottom of the screen, and fail contrast. So blocks only move into place, and only photos fade. A browser without support shows the page still.
Reduce motion
All of it is off, including parallax, hover lifts and the moving logos.
Keyboard
Hover lifts also show on keyboard focus. The moving logos stop while focus is inside them, and the pause button is a real button with at least 24 × 24 px of target (WCAG 2.5.8).
In the builder
The canvas stays still while you edit, so blocks don't jump under the pointer. The preview in Site settings and the published site show the motion.
