Media library

Today every image is a pasted web address. F034 lets you upload photos from your Mac or phone, keep them in one library, and pick them in any image field. Alt text is asked for when a photo comes in, and visitors get resized copies that suit their screen. Three ways to arrange it are below, plus one question about where the photos are stored.

A · Picker from the field

  • Works the same in every image field, including Gallery and Logo row items.
  • Big previews make choosing and writing alt text easy.
  • A familiar pattern (it's how YOOtheme and WordPress do it).
Best for: picking and describing photos. No dragging needed.Cost: a dialog covers the page while you choose.

B · Media tab in the sidebar

  • Photos are always one click away while you build.
  • Drag-and-drop feels quick on a Mac.
  • Thumbnails are small; alt text gets less room.
Best for: placing many photos fast.Cost: drag-first, and cramped on small screens and phones.

C · Media page + quick picker

  • A full page to tidy the library: alt text, unused photos, sizes.
  • Shows where each photo is used before you delete it.
  • Two places to learn instead of one.
Best for: managing a large library.Cost: more to build; the popover alone can't upload.
One question for you

Where should uploaded photos live?

Recommended

1 · In GitHub, next to your pages

Setup
None. It uses the GitHub access saving already has.
Cost
$0
Undo
Every upload is in the history, like your pages.
Limit
About 1 GB of photos (around 2,000 resized photos). Big photos are shrunk to 2400 px in your browser before upload.

2 · Cloudflare R2 storage

Setup
You turn on R2 in Cloudflare and add one more secret. Cloudflare asks for a payment method to turn R2 on, even for the free tier.
Cost
$0 up to 10 GB
Undo
Deleted photos are gone.
Limit
10 GB free.

Claude's recommendation: A, with photos in GitHub (1). It needs no new accounts, and it can move to R2 later without changing how the library looks. Either way, the published site gets resized copies (480 to 2400 px wide) with srcset, so phones download small files.
